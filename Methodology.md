# Methodology

## Overview

This investigation used a structured GEO-OSINT workflow focused on environmental and architectural reconstruction.

The objective was to approximate image capture time and scene orientation using:
- fixed geographic structures
- solar positioning
- road alignment
- environmental lighting behavior

without reliance on:
- metadata
- EXIF information
- embedded GPS coordinates
- timestamps

---

# Investigative Workflow

## 1. Landmark Identification

Visible text, architectural features, and surrounding structures were analyzed to identify recognizable geographic indicators.

This stage focused on:
- signage
- unique buildings
- structural layout
- urban characteristics

The objective was to establish a probable geographic region for further reconstruction.

---

# 2. Geographic Correlation

Once initial landmarks were identified, satellite maps and surrounding structural layouts were used to correlate the visible scene with real-world locations.

This process included:
- map comparison
- building placement verification
- surrounding road analysis
- urban layout matching

The purpose was to reconstruct the approximate spatial environment of the image.

---

# 3. Hemisphere and Solar Context Analysis

After geographic correlation, regional solar behavior was analyzed using hemisphere-based solar mechanics.

This included:
- expected sun path direction
- probable solar azimuth ranges
- seasonal lighting behavior
- midday solar positioning

The objective was to constrain possible lighting directions relative to the scene.

---

# 4. Structural Orientation Analysis

Building orientation and façade positioning were analyzed to determine how sunlight interacted with visible structures.

This stage focused on:
- entrance illumination
- façade exposure
- observer viewpoint geometry
- relative directional positioning

The objective was to infer probable scene orientation using architectural alignment.

---

# 5. Road Alignment Analysis

Visible roads and street layouts were compared against map orientation data.

This process included:
- east-west and north-south alignment estimation
- street geometry comparison
- structural adjacency analysis

Road orientation was used to strengthen directional consistency within the reconstruction.

---

# 6. Solar Simulation and Cross-Validation

Solar positioning simulations were performed using:

- SunCalc

The location was loaded into the solar model and multiple time intervals were tested against:
- observed lighting conditions
- façade illumination
- directional consistency
- reconstructed geometry

The purpose was to identify solar positions that most accurately matched the observed scene.

---

# 7. Environmental Lighting Interpretation

Environmental lighting characteristics were analyzed to estimate:
- solar elevation
- light warmth
- directional intensity
- probable daytime conditions

This stage focused on consistency between:
- observed illumination
- expected regional solar behavior
- reconstructed scene orientation

---

# 8. Confidence Assessment

Each major finding was evaluated using confidence-based assessment.

Factors considered included:
- landmark reliability
- geometric consistency
- solar alignment accuracy
- environmental agreement
- map correlation strength

This ensured that conclusions remained evidence-based and proportionate to available data.

---

# Methodological Principles

This investigation followed several core principles:

- Evidence before conclusion
- Multi-source validation
- Environmental consistency
- Geographic reproducibility
- Non-reliance on metadata
- Structured analytical reasoning

---

# Investigative Scope

The methodology is intended for:
- GEO-OSINT investigations
- environmental reconstruction
- image geolocation studies
- solar positioning analysis
- architectural orientation analysis
- open-source spatial investigations

The workflow is designed to remain reproducible using publicly available geographic and environmental data sources.
